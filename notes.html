01 / Technique
Binding

One less curl to fight.

A workshop post shows binding reverse-coiled inside a roll of tape to help straighten it. The tape roll becomes a small holding fixture for a material that wants to keep its curl.

That is a recurring theme in this shop: sometimes the useful tool is already sitting beside the thing you are working on.

“Reverse coiling binding inside a roll tape to straighten it”

02 / Useful scraps
Fixtures

The tool before the instrument.

Acrylic offcuts show up in the feed as material for router bases, templates, and jigs. Another post follows a router sled base. A side-dot jig and a modified fret-dressing stick have their own moments, too.

The work is not only shaping the instrument. It is also figuring out how to support it, locate the tool, and make a small operation easier to repeat.

“Acrylic scraps are usually free and great for making router bases, templates, and jigs.”

03 / Trial & adjustment
Experiments

Is it dumb if it works?

That question appears right on a Moon & Pearl title card. So do “3rd time’s the charm” and “Conclusion: not terrible.” The feed makes room for tool experiments, little improvements, and attempts that need another pass.

The interesting part is often the adjustment: adding a notch to a fret-dressing stick, changing how a piece is held, or making a fixture from what is available.
